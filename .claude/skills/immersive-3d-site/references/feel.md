# Feel

Energy and feeling are different things. Energy is how loud an act is: speed, scale, brightness. Feeling is
what the visitor experiences: curiosity, awe, calm, confidence, delight. A loud page the whole way through is
flat. A quiet act on a loud page can be the most powerful moment on it.

## 1. The feeling curve

Write it before choosing any scene devices. One line per beat:

```
hero      curiosity    a glass object turning in the dark, nothing explained yet
morph     anticipation noise pulling itself into a shape
rail      awe          flying through ninety rings at speed
explode   trust        the parts laid out, nothing hidden
close     calm         the object at rest, the price, one button
```

Rules:
- No two neighbours share a feeling. If they do, one of them is filler: cut or change it now, while it is cheap.
- A device chosen before its feeling is a device looking for a reason. Feeling first, device second.

## 2. The peak

One act is the moment people remember. Name it as the sentence a visitor would say to a friend:
*the bit where you climb to the top of the wall and the whole gym opens up underneath you.*

- It gets the most scroll room on the page, by a clear margin.
- It gets the best scene: the most work, the most detail.
- **The act before it is quieter.** Contrast is what makes it land. A big act after a big act is just more.
- Only one. Two peaks compete and neither wins.

## 3. Authored silence

A deliberate pause (a calm hold, a near-empty frame, one line of type) is a choice, not dead scroll. Mark it
in `BRIEF.md` as silence so the checks can tell it from a section where nothing happens by mistake.

## 4. The close

The last screen resolves and holds. It is the feeling the visitor leaves with, so it is never a fade to
nothing and never just a footer arriving. Something settles, the action is clear, and it stays put.

## 5. The tell-someone sentence

Finish this in `BRIEF.md`: *It's the site where ___.* Fill it with an experience, not a technique.
"It's the site where the speaker comes apart around you" works. "It's the site with an exploded view" does not.

## 6. The feel check

After the screenshot checks pass, and before reporting:

1. Scroll the page cold, top to bottom, without opening `BRIEF.md`.
2. Write one word per act for what you actually felt.
3. Now open `BRIEF.md` and compare with the intended curve.
4. Where they disagree, the page is wrong, not the brief. Fix the page.
5. On the contact sheet, confirm the peak is the biggest visual change and has the most scroll room, and that
   the close holds.

Put the comparison (intended against felt, and what you changed) in the report.
